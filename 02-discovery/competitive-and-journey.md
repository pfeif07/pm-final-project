# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Role: A consumer with several open claims and little financial slack, who is likely completing the journey on her phone during a break.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Goal: To set up one plan she can actually afford, without having to phone anyone.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Friction: She has a bad moment at the plan choice. The portal presents the options as if one is the “right answer” (N-02), so she picks the shortest term, finds the amount unaffordable and misses the second payment. The reminder before her first instalment can also arrive after the due date (T-207), and the chat assistant confirms plans without explaining what happens if she pays late (T-208, N-05).
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** get the neded documents
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** She hits the plan choice and can’t tell which option fits her income. The portal reads as if one option is the “right answer” (N-02), and nothing explains why income is requested or how it’s used (T-206, N-06).
She holds off on agreeing. The summary says consumers withhold agreement until someone explains a discrepancy (T-203, N-07), such as a portal amount that doesn’t match the letter.
She phones an agent, because some consumers trust a person to tell them whether a decision will cost more (N-04, N-07).
The agent starts from scratch. Roughly half of calls begin by repeating details she already entered online, and the agent can’t see her answers or the plan rationale (T-205, N-08, N-09).
If she doesn’t call, she takes the other path in the summary: she either abandons the journey (N-01) or accepts the fast chat confirmation without learning the cost of paying late (T-208, N-05).
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** For Mara, it defeats her goal of not phoning anyone, and she has to re-explain her situation. If she tries the digital route again, the back button clears her affordability answers (T-204), so she re-enters sensitive financial data.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Broken plans: Mara’s unassisted path ends in a missed second payment. A late reminder (T-207) and no explanation of consequences (T-208) make that likelier. The summary reports rising broken plans in client reporting (N-10), and complaint volume is a client success measure.
